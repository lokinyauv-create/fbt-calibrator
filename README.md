Full-body tracker calibration for **SteamVR on Linux**, inspired by the calibration part of [Standable](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2370570/). It does **not** estimate a body from the headset. It only straightens and calibrates the hardware trackers you already have.

- **T-pose calibration.** Stand straight with your **feet together** and arms out for one second. You hear a camera click. Your waist and foot trackers are now "straight": both feet are symmetric and sit at the ankles, however the trackers are strapped on.
- **Saved.** Strap the trackers the same way next time and you don't need to calibrate again.
- **VRChat calibration in any pose.** Press *Calibrate FBT* in VRChat while sitting or lying down. The trackers are held on a perfect T-pose for that moment, so you just pull the trigger.
- **Trackers stick to the avatar.** While VRChat waits for the trigger, the trackers follow your head, so turning or looking down doesn't move them off the avatar. They are placed for the height VRChat uses, so they land on the avatar's hips and feet.
- **Height check.** If VRChat's *Real height* is far from the height measured in the T-pose, the window tells you which number to set.
- **Feet width** for VRChat calibration (*Feet closer / wider*). Use it if the avatar's thighs splay out or cross.
- **Works with mixed setups.** A Quest headset with Lighthouse trackers, lined up by Space Calibrator, works too (see [below](#quest--lighthouse-space-calibrator)).
- Optional dots in VR showing where the calibrated trackers are.
- **Update button.** It installs the latest GitHub release.

## Install in one step

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lokinyauv-create/fbt-calibrator/main/get.sh | bash
```

Then **restart SteamVR once** so the driver loads. Open **Tracker Calibration** from the app menu. It also starts by itself at login, or together with SteamVR if you use [VR Auto Launcher](https://github.com/lokinyauv-create/vr-autolauncher).

To remove it: `~/.local/share/fbt-calibrator/uninstall.sh`

Needs: Linux, native SteamVR, `python3`. The installer fetches `openvr`, `numpy` and `PySide6` with pip if your system doesn't have them. A prebuilt driver is included. Building it needs `cmake` and `g++`.

## Use it

1. **Calibrate once:** stand straight, feet **together** (touching), look ahead and hold your arms out (T-pose). After about 1 s you hear a camera click 📸. A warning sound means a tracker wasn't visible or your feet were apart.
2. **VRChat:** turn on **Settings → Tracking & IK → IK Debug Logging** and **One Handed Calibration**. Then press *Calibrate FBT* in any pose. You hear a bell 🔔 and the trackers stand straight. Pull the trigger, and you hear a "done" sound when the trackers follow you again.
3. If the window shows a height warning, set that **Real height** in VRChat (Settings → Tracking & IK).
4. If the legs look wrong, change the **feet width** in the window and calibrate in VRChat again.

Untick *Calibrate with a T-pose* if you don't want accidental recalibration.

## Quest + Lighthouse (Space Calibrator)

Tested on 2026-10-28 with this setup, and it works:

- **Quest 3** through **Steam Link** (SteamVR 2.18.4, Linux)
- **Index controllers** and **4× VIVE Tracker 3.0**: one strapped to the headset, plus waist and both feet
- **[Space Calibrator](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3368750/) Nova v2.0.1-BETA**, the native Linux build from Steam, lining Lighthouse up with the Quest

What to know:

- Calibrate Space Calibrator first, then do the T-pose. In Space Calibrator, **Edit Calibration** should show pitch and roll within a few degrees. Large values like 30° or 100° mean a bad calibration that throws every Lighthouse device away from you. Press **Clear Calibration** and do it again.
- The tracker strapped to the headset is skipped automatically: anything within 30 cm of your eyes is not a body tracker. Tick **Hide target device from apps** in Space Calibrator so VRChat doesn't see it either.
- If you installed an older OpenVR-SpaceCalibrator driver yourself into `~/.local/share/SteamVR/drivers/01spacecalibrator`, remove it. It hides the driver bundled with the Steam version, which then says *Failed to connect to the Space Calibrator SteamVR driver*.

## How it works

- `driver/` is a tiny SteamVR driver (`fbtcal`). It hooks `IVRServerDriverHost::TrackedDevicePoseUpdated` and rewrites the **real** trackers' poses in place. It adds no virtual trackers and hides nothing. The calibration offset is folded into the pose's driver-from-head transform, so SteamVR's own prediction stays correct. The driver publishes the untouched poses and reads the offsets through shared memory (`/dev/shm/fbtcal-v1`).
- `app/` is the tray app (Python, PySide6, pyopenvr). It handles T-pose detection, the body model, saving, VRChat log watching and the in-VR dots.
- Other drivers can move trackers after ours. Space Calibrator does this, and our raw poses were 3.85 m and 153° away from the Quest headset. So the app works in the space games see: it takes its own device-local offset back off the final pose. It also measures what the other drivers add, so the frozen VRChat T-pose still lands in the right place.
- VRChat detection reads VRChat's own log (IK Debug Logging): `Calibrate FBT and reload avatar…` / `Preparing for calibration…` start it, and `Restore tracking…` ends it.
- Files live in `~/.config/fbt-calibrator/`: the calibration, settings and a log.
- A watch panel for [VRN, my WayVR build](https://github.com/lokinyauv-create/wayvr) reads `~/.cache/fbt-calibrator/status.json` and drops commands into `~/.cache/fbt-calibrator/commands/`.
